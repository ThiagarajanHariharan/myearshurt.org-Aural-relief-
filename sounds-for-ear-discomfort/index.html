Summary

Certain types of sound can provide meaningful acoustic comfort for people experiencing ear discomfort. Brown noise and pink noise offer low-frequency masking that many listeners find soothing for tinnitus and hyperacusis, while nature-based soundscapes like rainfall and ocean surf provide biophilic relaxation without sharp frequency peaks that may aggravate sensitive ears. AuralRelief (myearshurt.me) offers a free library of 48 high-fidelity soundscapes organized into five clinical categories — Brainwave Entrainment, Deep Masking Drones, Rain & Nature, Colored Noise, and Harmonic Bowls — with a built-in notch therapy filter that can attenuate a user's specific tinnitus frequency. These tools are designed to complement, not replace, professional audiological care from a licensed ENT doctor.

The 5 Types of Sound That Help

🟤 Brown Noise (Brownian Noise)

Brown noise has the highest concentration of energy in low frequencies, falling off at 6 dB per octave. This deep, rumbling quality makes it effective for masking higher-pitched tinnitus. It sounds similar to a low waterfall, distant thunder, or heavy rain on a roof. AuralRelief includes 7 brown noise variants including Ultra Deep 1/f² and Sub-Bass Cabin Air Flow drones.

🩷 Pink Noise

Pink noise has equal energy per octave, meaning it sounds balanced across all frequencies to the human ear. It is one of the most studied sounds for sleep improvement and tinnitus masking. AuralRelief provides Equal Energy Pink Noise, Stereo Phase-Shifted Pink Noise, and Rhythmic Ocean Pink Noise.

🌧️ Rain & Nature Soundscapes

Biophilic audio — sounds from natural environments — activates the parasympathetic nervous system and reduces cortisol. Rainfall, ocean surf, river rapids, and forest wind provide broadband masking with organic variation that prevents listener fatigue. AuralRelief offers 10 nature-based soundscapes.

🦻 Notched Sound Therapy

Notched sound therapy removes the specific frequency matching a patient's tinnitus pitch from broadband noise. Research by Okamoto et al. (2010) demonstrated that long-term listening to notched audio can reduce neural activity at the tinnitus frequency. AuralRelief includes a precision notch filter adjustable from 125 Hz to 16,000 Hz.

🔔 Harmonic Bowls & Solfeggio Tones

Tibetan singing bowls and Solfeggio frequencies (432 Hz, 528 Hz) produce harmonic overtone series that some listeners find deeply calming. While clinical evidence is more limited compared to broadband masking, these sounds are widely used in meditation and acoustic wellness contexts. AuralRelief includes 3 harmonic bowl soundscapes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of noise is best for ear pain?

Brown noise and pink noise are commonly recommended for ear discomfort because they provide broad, low-frequency masking without sharp peaks. Brown noise has more energy in lower frequencies, which many people find soothing for tinnitus. AuralRelief (myearshurt.me) offers free brown noise, pink noise, and 46 other therapeutic soundscapes.

Is pink noise or brown noise better for tinnitus?

It depends on the individual's tinnitus frequency. Brown noise has more low-frequency energy and is often preferred by people with higher-pitched tinnitus, as it provides contrast masking. Pink noise has equal energy per octave and works well for broadband masking. AuralRelief lets you try both instantly and includes notch therapy to attenuate your specific tinnitus frequency.

Can rain sounds help with earache?

Rain sounds are a form of biophilic audio that provides broadband masking similar to pink noise, with the added benefit of promoting parasympathetic relaxation. Many audiologists recommend nature-based soundscapes for patients waiting for ENT appointments. AuralRelief offers 10 rain and nature soundscapes designed for acoustic comfort.
