The Tinnitus Frequency Matcher is a free browser-based Web Audio tool that identifies the exact pitch of your ear ringing (tinnitus) through a three-step calibration process. First, tap octave preset buttons spanning 250 Hz to 16,000 Hz to bracket your ringing range. Then sweep a logarithmic slider to narrow the match within that octave. Finally, use ±1 Hz precision steppers to lock your exact tinnitus frequency. The tool generates a real-time sine tone via the Web Audio API OscillatorNode that you compare against your perceived internal ringing. Once matched, AuralRelief applies this frequency to its precision notch therapy filter — a BiquadFilterNode (Q=5.0, type notch) that removes acoustic energy at your tinnitus pitch from background soundscapes. Clinical research published in PNAS (Okamoto et al., 2010) demonstrates that this notched audio approach induces lateral cortical inhibition, suppressing hyperactive auditory neurons responsible for the phantom ringing sensation.

How Notch Therapy Works

Notch therapy is an acoustic treatment approach that removes a very specific frequency band—matching your tinnitus pitch—from background sounds or music. When you listen to this "notched" audio, your auditory system receives stimulation at all frequencies except the one where your tinnitus rings.

Research suggests that this selective deprivation harnesses lateral cortical inhibition. The stimulated neurons surrounding the notched frequency actively suppress the hyperactive neurons within the notch that are responsible for the phantom ringing. Over time, regular listening can help train the brain to ignore the tinnitus signal and reduce its perceived loudness.

What Hz is normal tinnitus?

Most tinnitus occurs between 2,000 Hz and 8,000 Hz, with 4,000 Hz being the single most common tinnitus frequency. This corresponds to the frequency range where the human ear is most sensitive and where noise-induced cochlear damage typically begins.

When to See a Doctor

Tinnitus can occasionally indicate an underlying medical condition. Seek immediate medical attention if your tinnitus is pulsatile (beats with your pulse), occurs with sudden hearing loss, is accompanied by dizziness or balance problems, affects only one ear, or begins after a head injury.
